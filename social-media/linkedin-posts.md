These aren't "nice to have" skills. They're survival skills.

ProjectAdulting was built to close this gap — free, accessible lessons on the things that actually matter after graduation.

**Post 3 — Lessons from Building**

3 things I learned building an education app:

1. People don't want courses. They want answers.
Nobody wants to sit through a 2-hour lecture on taxes. They want to know: "How do I file? What forms do I need? How long does it take?" Short, actionable content wins every time.

2. The demand is massive but invisible.
Millions of people quietly Google "how does credit work" or "how to cook chicken." They don't post about it. They don't ask for help. They just struggle in silence. Building for this audience means meeting people where they are.

3. Simplicity is the product.
The information is already out there. The value isn't in the content itself — it's in making it clear, organized, and approachable. That's harder than it sounds.

If you're building in edtech or working on financial literacy initiatives, I'd love to connect.

## Thought Leadership

**Post 4 — Education Gap**

We teach students how to analyze Shakespeare but not how to analyze a pay stub.

We teach the Pythagorean theorem but not the 50/30/20 budgeting rule.

We teach the periodic table but not how to read a lease.

I'm not saying those subjects don't matter. But practical life skills shouldn't be treated as optional content.

Financial literacy. Basic cooking. Understanding taxes and credit. These should be foundational, not afterthoughts.

Until the system changes, tools like ProjectAdulting exist to fill the gap.

**Post 5 — Financial Literacy Advocacy**

The average American carries $6,000+ in credit card debt.

Not because they're irresponsible. Because nobody taught them how credit works.

When you don't understand interest rates, minimum payments, or how utilization affects your score — debt happens fast.

Financial literacy isn't about being good with money. It's about being informed. And that starts with education.

That's exactly what we're building at ProjectAdulting — making financial concepts accessible to everyone, starting with the basics.

Because you can't make good financial decisions with bad financial education.

## Posting Tips for LinkedIn

1. **Write in short paragraphs.** One sentence per line performs best. Use white space liberally.
2. **Hook in the first 2 lines.** LinkedIn truncates after ~3 lines with "...see more." Make the opening count.
3. **Be personal, not corporate.** First-person stories outperform branded announcements.
4. **Post 2-3 times per week.** Consistency matters more than frequency.
5. **Engage in comments.** Reply to every comment within the first hour — it boosts the algorithm.
6. **Best posting times.** Tuesday-Thursday, 8-10 AM in your target audience's time zone.
7. **No external links in the main post.** LinkedIn suppresses posts with links. Put the link in the first comment or edit it in after posting.
8. **Use 3-5 hashtags.** Mix broad (#Education) with niche (#ProjectAdulting).
